There is no single best YouTube niche. There is a best niche for what you want: the most money per view, the fastest growth, or the easiest start. In 2026 the niches that balance all three are **software tutorials**, **exam prep and study skills**, **careers and interviews**, **insurance and legal explainers**, **language learning** and **local real estate**. Each one pays at least a middle rate per view, faces moderate or low competition, and has search demand that lasts for years.

Most "best niches" lists rank on one of those three things and ignore the other two. This guide compares 26 niches on all three, picks the best for each goal, shows how each niche earns beyond ad revenue, and gives you a way to test a niche before you film fifty videos in it.

## The three things that decide a niche

**Pay per view.** Advertisers pay more to reach viewers who are about to spend money on something valuable. A viewer researching an investment account, business software or insurance is worth more to an advertiser than one watching a game walkthrough. Pay also depends on where viewers live, and ad rates usually rise late in the year when retailers spend more. [YouTube CPM by niche](../youtube-cpm-by-niche/) explains this in detail.

**Competition.** A niche where the top results are huge channels with years of videos is hard to break into, however well it pays. What matters is not how many channels exist, but whether the specific questions viewers ask already have strong answers.

**Effort.** Some videos take an afternoon of screen recording. Others take a week of travel, filming and editing. The niche you can keep up for two years beats the one you abandon after two months.

## Most profitable YouTube niches

If ad pay per view is your priority, the high-pay niches are **personal finance and investing**, **B2B software and SaaS reviews**, **insurance and legal explainers** and **real estate**. They pay well because the viewers are close to expensive decisions, and advertisers compete to reach them.

Each has a catch:

- **Personal finance** is the best known and the most crowded. Viewers want to trust the person giving money advice, so it is hard to do faceless, and the big finance channels are very big. A narrow audience works better: finance for nurses, for freelancers, or for people in one country.
- **B2B software reviews** have small audiences and very high value per viewer. A video with a few thousand views can earn more from one software sponsor or affiliate sale than a viral video earns from ads.
- **Insurance and legal explainers** pay well and face little competition, because the subjects are dry and hard to make watchable. That difficulty is the opportunity. Be careful with anything that sounds like personal advice, and say clearly that you are explaining, not advising.
- **Real estate** is crowded nationally and almost empty locally. A channel about buying property in one city or region competes with a handful of local agents, not with national stars.

"Profitable" also means income beyond ads. A low-pay niche can still be very profitable if it sells well, which is covered below.

## Best niches if you want to grow fastest

Fast growth comes from **high search demand with weak current answers**. These niches often have that:

- **Exam prep and study skills.** Every exam season brings a new wave of searches, and viewers are loyal for as long as they are studying.
- **Software tutorials for newly popular tools.** Every new release or tool creates questions that no one has answered well yet.
- **AI tools and automation.** Crowded at the top, but every new tool resets the race in its corner. Speed matters more than polish.
- **Specific sub-categories of tech reviews.** "Tech reviews" is crowded. "Budget microphones for podcasters" is not.

The trade-off is that fast-moving topics date quickly. A tutorial for a tool's current version needs updating when the next one ships.

## Best niches for faceless channels

Faceless works where the screen, the hands or the story is the point:

- **Software tutorials and coding**, recorded on screen.
- **Insurance and legal explainers**, with diagrams, text and voice-over.
- **True stories and documentaries**, built on narration, footage and research. Scripting is slow work.
- **Craft and making**, filmed from above.
- **Cooking and recipes**, filmed as hands and food.
- **Gaming**, where the game is on screen.

Faceless struggles where trust in a person is the point, such as finance advice, fitness coaching, parenting and careers advice. Faceless channels must also be careful with YouTube's inauthentic content policy: mass-produced, repetitive videos with little original input can lose monetization. A faceless channel still needs original scripts, research and editing. [YouTube demonetization](../youtube-demonetization/) explains the policy.

## Most popular niches, and why popular is not the same as good

The biggest channels on YouTube are mostly music labels, children's entertainment and large entertainment brands. Our [most subscribed channels](../../creator-tools/youtube-most-subscribed-channels/) list shows the current leaders. Those niches are popular because huge numbers of people watch them, but that does not make them good niches to start in. They are dominated by companies with large budgets, and children's content carries extra rules and lower ad options under YouTube's made-for-kids settings.

For a new creator, the useful question is not "what is popular?" but "what do people search for that is not answered well yet?"

## Niches to start only with a clear angle

- **Gaming walkthroughs.** The easiest to start, the hardest to stand out in, and the lowest pay per view. Gaming news and analysis is faster to make and easier to differentiate.
- **Make money online.** Saturated, and viewers have learned to distrust it. You need results you can show.
- **Fitness.** Enormous demand and enormous supply. A narrow audience, such as home workouts for one specific group, does better than general fitness.
- **Travel.** Lovely to watch, expensive to make, and poorly paid per view. It works best with a specific angle, such as budget travel from one country.
- **Film and TV analysis.** Watch out for copyright. Commentary needs real transformation of the footage you use. [Copyright claim vs strike](../youtube-copyright-claim-vs-strike/) explains the risks.

## Test a niche before you commit

Ratings are a starting point. This test tells you whether a niche works for you.

1. **Check demand.** Put your topic into the [YouTube keyword generator](../../creator-tools/youtube-keyword-generator/). If people search for dozens of specific questions about it, there is room for a channel. If you only find a handful of broad phrases, the niche may be too small or too vague.
2. **Check the competition.** Search those questions on YouTube. If the top results are old, short, low quality or from small channels, that is an opening. If every question is answered by large channels with recent videos, look for a narrower angle.
3. **Check for breakouts.** Look for small channels in the niche whose videos get far more views than their subscriber count. That shows YouTube is willing to recommend newcomers there. Our [outliers feed](../../research/outliers/) lists videos doing many times their channel's usual views, updated daily.
4. **List 30 ideas.** If you cannot list 30 videos you could make, the niche is too narrow or not one you know well enough. The [video ideas generator](../../creator-tools/youtube-video-ideas-generator/) helps fill gaps.
5. **Make five videos.** Five is enough to learn whether you enjoy it and whether anyone watches. It is not enough to judge long-term success, so judge your own enjoyment more than the views.

### An example

Here is how the test might go for someone who knows spreadsheets well.

"Excel tutorials" is a crowded topic. The keyword generator shows people asking very specific questions: how to use a particular formula, how to build a budget template, how to fix a specific error. Searching those shows many answers are long, old videos or screen recordings without explanation. So the angle becomes "Excel for small business owners: one problem solved in under ten minutes". It is faceless, low effort, middle pay, and has an obvious product to sell later: templates. Listing 30 ideas is easy, because every common small-business task is a video.

## The niche matters less than the angle

Most successful channels did not find an empty niche. They found an angle on a crowded one: a specific audience, a specific format, or a point of view nobody else had. "Cooking" is crowded. "Cooking for one on a student budget" is a channel. "Fitness" is crowded. "Strength training for people over 60" is a channel.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best YouTube niche in 2026?

There is no single best niche. For a balance of pay, competition and effort, software tutorials, exam prep, careers and interviews, insurance and legal explainers, language learning and local real estate stand out. The best one for you depends on your goal and what you can make consistently.

### What are the most profitable YouTube niches?

For ad pay per view, personal finance and investing, B2B software reviews, insurance and legal explainers and real estate rank highest, because advertisers pay most to reach viewers making expensive decisions. Counting sponsorships and products, niches like software tutorials, language learning and craft can also be very profitable.

### What are the most popular niches on YouTube?

By audience size, music, children's entertainment, gaming and general entertainment dominate. Popular is not the same as a good place to start, because those niches are crowded with large channels and companies.

### What is the best niche for a faceless YouTube channel?

Software tutorials, coding, insurance and legal explainers, true stories and documentaries, craft and cooking all work well faceless, because the screen, the hands or the story is the point. Faceless channels still need original scripts and editing to stay within YouTube's inauthentic content rules.

### What is the easiest YouTube niche to start?

Low-effort niches include software tutorials, careers advice, exam prep, productivity and gaming. Gaming is the easiest to start but the hardest to stand out in and the lowest paid per view.

### Can I change my niche later?

Yes, but it costs some momentum. YouTube recommends videos to viewers similar to those who watched before, so a sudden change means finding a new audience. Moving to a related niche, or narrowing your current one, is easier than a complete switch.

### How do I find my niche?

List what you know well, what you could make 30 videos about, and what you would enjoy making for two years. Then check search demand and competition for each with a keyword tool and YouTube search. The [niche finder](../../creator-tools/youtube-niche-finder/) ranks niches against your goal to narrow the list.

### Is it too late to start a YouTube channel in 2026?

No. YouTube says it judges each video on its own performance, not on the channel's history, so a well-made video from a new channel can be recommended. What has changed is that broad topics are crowded, so a specific angle matters more than it used to.
