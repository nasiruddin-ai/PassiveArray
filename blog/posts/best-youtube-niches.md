There is no single best YouTube niche. There is a best niche for what you want: the most money per view, the fastest growth, or the easiest start. Most lists rank niches on one of those and ignore the other two. This one compares all three, and then shows you how to test a niche before you film fifty videos in it.

The ratings below come from our [YouTube niche finder](../../creator-tools/youtube-niche-finder/), which ranks the same niches against your own goal. They are an editorial read of public creator reports and of what currently ranks, not measured data.

## The three things that decide a niche

**Pay per view.** Advertisers pay more to reach viewers who are about to spend money on something valuable. Finance and business software sit at the top, gaming and entertainment at the bottom. [YouTube CPM by niche](../youtube-cpm-by-niche/) explains why.

**Competition.** A niche where the top results are huge channels with years of videos is hard to break into, however well it pays.

**Effort.** Some videos take an afternoon of screen recording. Others take a week of travel, filming and editing. The niche you can keep up for two years beats the one you abandon after two months.

## Niches that balance all three

| Niche | Pay | Competition | Effort | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Software tutorials | Middle | Moderate | Low | Screen recording only, and search demand lasts for years |
| Insurance and legal explainers | High | Low | High | Dry subjects that almost nobody makes watchable |
| Exam prep and study skills | Middle | Low | Low | Seasonal spikes and very loyal viewers |
| Careers and interviews | Middle | Moderate | Low | Evergreen demand and sponsors in recruitment and courses |
| Real estate, local angle | High | High nationally, low locally | High | Local channels face almost no competition |
| Language learning | Middle | Moderate | Moderate | Evergreen search and an obvious course to sell |

## Best niches if you want the most money per view

Personal finance and investing, B2B software reviews, insurance and legal explainers, and real estate. The catch is trust: viewers want to know who is giving them money advice, so these are hard to do faceless, and the big finance channels are very big.

## Best niches if you want to grow fastest

Look for high search demand with weak current answers. Exam prep, software tutorials for newly popular tools, and specific sub-categories of tech reviews often have plenty of viewers and few good videos. Every new AI tool or software release resets the race in its corner.

## Best niches for faceless channels

Software tutorials, coding, insurance and legal explainers, true stories and documentaries, and craft and making. Faceless works where the screen, the hands or the story is the point. It struggles where trust in a person is the point, such as finance advice or fitness coaching.

## Niches to start only with a clear angle

- **Gaming walkthroughs.** The easiest to start, the hardest to stand out in, and the lowest pay per view.
- **Make money online.** Saturated, and viewers have learned to distrust it. You need results you can show.
- **Fitness.** Enormous demand and enormous supply. A narrow audience, such as home workouts for one specific group, does better than general fitness.
- **Travel.** Lovely to watch, expensive to make, and poorly paid per view.

## Test a niche before you commit

1. **Check demand.** Put your topic into the [YouTube keyword generator](../../creator-tools/youtube-keyword-generator/). If people search for dozens of specific questions about it, there is room for a channel.
2. **Check the competition.** Search those questions on YouTube. If the top results are old, short or from small channels, that is an opening.
3. **List 30 ideas.** If you cannot list 30 videos you could make, the niche is too narrow or not one you know well enough. The [video ideas generator](../../creator-tools/youtube-video-ideas-generator/) helps fill gaps.
4. **Make five videos.** Five is enough to learn whether you enjoy it and whether anyone watches. It is not enough to judge long-term success, so judge your enjoyment more than the views.

## The niche matters less than the angle

Most successful channels did not find an empty niche. They found an angle on a crowded one: a specific audience, a specific format, or a point of view nobody else had. "Cooking" is crowded. "Cooking for one on a student budget" is a channel.
