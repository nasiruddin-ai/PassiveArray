A long-form YouTube video with 4 million views usually earns somewhere between **$2,000 and $16,000** from ads. That is 4,000 thousands of views multiplied by an RPM of $0.50 to $4.00, the default range in our [YouTube money calculator](../../creator-tools/youtube-money-calculator/). If the video is a Short, the figure is a small fraction of that. If the channel is not in the YouTube Partner Program, it is zero.

## The arithmetic

> Earnings = (views / 1,000) x RPM

RPM is what the creator keeps per 1,000 views, after YouTube's share and after counting every view that showed no ad. For 4 million views:

| RPM | Earnings on 4,000,000 views |
|---|---|
| $0.50 | $2,000 |
| $1.00 | $4,000 |
| $2.00 | $8,000 |
| $4.00 | $16,000 |
| $10.00 | $40,000 |

The last row is not a typo. Finance, business software and insurance channels can run RPMs well above the usual high end, because advertisers pay far more to reach those viewers. Most channels never see it.

## Four realistic scenarios

The same 4 million views can be worth very different amounts. These are the patterns that decide it.

**A viral entertainment video with a global audience.** Views come from everywhere, many in countries where advertisers pay little. Expect the bottom of the range, around $2,000 to $4,000.

**A long tutorial watched mostly in the US, UK, Canada and Australia.** The audience is in the highest-paying ad markets and the video is long enough for mid-roll ads. Expect the upper half, $8,000 to $16,000 or more.

**A Short with 4 million views.** Shorts are paid from a shared pool, not from ads on your own video, and the per-view figure is far lower. A 4 million view Short usually earns an order of magnitude or more below the long-form figures above, and how far below depends on music use and where the viewers are. [How YouTube pays for Shorts](../how-much-does-youtube-pay-for-shorts/) explains why.

**A video on a channel that is not monetized.** No Partner Program, no ad revenue. Check this first with the [monetization checker](../../creator-tools/youtube-monetization-checker/), because a big view count says nothing about it.

## Why viral videos often earn less per view

A video that breaks out of its usual audience reaches people who would never search for it: international viewers, younger viewers, people scrolling past on the home feed. Those views are worth less to advertisers than a core audience that came for the subject. So a channel's breakout hit frequently has a lower RPM than its regular uploads, even while it earns the most in total.

## When the money arrives

Views on a viral video do not all land in one month. Most come in the first weeks, then a long tail continues for months or years. YouTube pays monthly for the previous month's finalized earnings, so a video that hit 4 million views over six weeks is paid across two or three payments. [How and when YouTube pays you](../how-does-youtube-pay-you/) has the schedule.

## 4 million views is often worth more off YouTube

At this scale the ad revenue is rarely the biggest number. A brand would price an integration on a video expected to reach millions at a placement rate far above ad RPM. The [sponsorship price calculator](../../creator-tools/youtube-sponsorship-price-calculator/) shows what a channel with that reach could charge per video. Merchandise, memberships and affiliate links all scale with views too.
