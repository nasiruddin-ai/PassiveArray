Search for the best time to post on YouTube and you will find confident answers: Thursday at 3 pm, weekends at noon. Most come from small samples of other people's channels, in other time zones, with other audiences. Your audience is not theirs. The answer that holds up is in your own YouTube Studio.

## Why publish time matters less than people think

YouTube recommends videos to people when they are using YouTube, not only at the moment you publish. A good video keeps getting suggested for days, weeks or years. Publish time mostly affects the first few hours: who sees it first, and how quickly those first viewers react.

That first window still matters, because early viewers' response helps YouTube decide who to show the video to next. So the goal is simple: have the video live just before your audience is most active.

## Where to find your audience's hours

In YouTube Studio, open **Analytics**, then the **Audience** tab. The card called **When your viewers are on YouTube** shows a heat map of days and hours, based on the last 28 days, in your local time. The darkest squares are when the most of your viewers are on YouTube.

The card only appears once a channel has enough viewers. If you do not see it yet, your audience is too small for the pattern to mean much, and consistency matters far more than timing.

## How to pick your publish time

1. **Find the darkest block** on the heat map.
2. **Publish one to two hours before it starts.** That gives YouTube time to process the video and start testing it before the peak arrives.
3. **Use scheduled publishing** in Studio so the video goes live at that time without you being there.
4. **Pick a day you can repeat.** A regular day and time trains your audience to expect you, which is worth more than finding the perfect slot once.

## Test it instead of trusting it

Keep the same publish time for four to six videos, then try another for the same number. Compare the views in the first 48 hours on each. One video proves nothing, because topic and thumbnail move views far more than the hour does.

## Shorts are different

Shorts are shown in a feed that surfaces older Shorts all the time, so publish time matters even less. Consistency of posting matters more than the hour.

## What actually moves views more than timing

- **The topic.** A video people are searching for will find them whenever you post it. The [video ideas generator](../../creator-tools/youtube-video-ideas-generator/) helps here.
- **The thumbnail and title.** They decide whether the people YouTube shows it to click. See [YouTube thumbnail size](../youtube-thumbnail-size/) for the basics.
- **Consistency.** A channel that posts on a schedule grows steadier than one that posts at the perfect time once a month.
