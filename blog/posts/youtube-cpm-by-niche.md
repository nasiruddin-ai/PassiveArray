Two channels with the same views can earn completely different amounts, and the subject of the videos is one of the biggest reasons. Advertisers pay more to reach some viewers than others, and the niche is how YouTube's ad system tells them apart. This guide explains why, which niches tend to pay most, and how to find the number for your own channel.

A note before the lists: there is no official table of YouTube CPM by niche. YouTube does not publish one, and the charts you see elsewhere are compiled from creators' screenshots. We group niches into bands below and say plainly that the bands are an editorial read, not measured data.

## CPM and RPM, briefly

**CPM** is what advertisers pay per 1,000 ad impressions. **RPM** is what you keep per 1,000 views after YouTube's share and after the views that showed no ad. Niche moves both, but RPM is the number that reaches your bank account. [How much YouTube pays per view](../how-much-does-youtube-pay-per-view/) explains the gap between them.

## Why the niche changes what advertisers pay

Advertisers bid in an auction for each ad slot. What they are willing to bid depends on what a viewer is worth to them.

- **Value of a customer.** A bank, a software company or an insurer can earn thousands from one customer, so it can afford to pay a lot to reach a likely buyer. A mobile game earns a few dollars per player.
- **Buying intent.** Someone watching "best credit card for travel" is close to a decision. Someone watching a funny compilation is not.
- **Competition between advertisers.** Where many companies chase the same viewers, the auction price rises.
- **Brand safety.** Advertisers avoid content they see as risky. Niches with frequent strong language, violence or sensitive topics attract fewer bidders.

## High, middle and low bands

These bands match the ones in our [YouTube niche finder](../../creator-tools/youtube-niche-finder/), which is built from public creator reports and what currently ranks. Treat them as a starting point.

| Band | Niches that usually sit here | Why |
|---|---|---|
| High | Personal finance and investing, B2B software, insurance and legal explainers, real estate | High customer value and strong buying intent |
| Middle | Tech reviews, software tutorials, AI tools, careers, home improvement, parenting, nutrition, language learning | Commercial products to sell, but lower value per customer |
| Low | Gaming, music, travel, cooking, fitness, entertainment, film commentary | Huge audiences, cheap products or few advertisers per viewer |

Kids' content sits below all three for a separate reason: videos made for kids cannot show personalized ads, which pay much more than contextual ones.

## Niche is not the only factor

A high-band niche does not guarantee a high CPM. The other big factors are:

1. **Audience country.** A finance channel watched mostly in a low-paying ad market can earn less per view than a gaming channel watched in the US. Country often outweighs niche.
2. **Season.** Advertisers spend most from October to December and least in January.
3. **Video length.** Videos of eight minutes or more can carry mid-roll ads, which means more impressions per view.
4. **Advertiser-friendliness.** Videos with limited ads earn a fraction of their potential, whatever the niche.

## How to find your real CPM by topic

Your own numbers beat any chart. In **YouTube Studio**, open **Analytics**, then **Revenue**, then **Advanced mode**, and look at playback-based CPM by video. Group your videos by topic and compare. Many creators find one topic in their catalogue earns two or three times the rest, and that is the most useful thing to know when choosing what to make next.

If you are not monetized yet, look at which topics competitors cover and use the [YouTube keyword generator](../../creator-tools/youtube-keyword-generator/) to see whether people search for the commercial angle of your subject: reviews, comparisons, "best" and "how to choose".

## Should you change niche for a higher CPM?

Usually not on its own. A high-CPM niche you cannot make well, or cannot keep making for years, earns less than a mid-CPM niche you are good at. The better move is often to cover the commercial angle of your own subject: a fitness channel reviewing equipment, a gaming channel reviewing hardware, a cooking channel comparing kitchen tools. Those videos attract the advertisers your other videos do not.
