YouTube views come from a few places: the Home feed, suggested videos beside other videos, search, Shorts, and your own subscribers and links. Getting more views means finding which of those is failing for your channel and fixing that one. Twelve fixes follow, grouped by the problem they solve.

One thing first: do not buy views. Paid view services break YouTube's fake engagement policy, the views are usually removed, and they teach YouTube's recommendations to show your video to people who never watch it, which lowers your reach afterwards.

## Find the problem before fixing it

Open **YouTube Studio**, go to **Analytics**, then **Content**, and look at two numbers for your recent videos.

- **Impressions** is how many times YouTube showed your thumbnail. Low impressions means YouTube is not offering your video to many people.
- **Impressions click-through rate** is how many of those people clicked. A low CTR means the title and thumbnail are not convincing the people who see them.

Then check **average view duration** on each video. If people click but leave fast, YouTube stops recommending the video. [YouTube Analytics explained](../youtube-analytics-explained/) walks through each metric.

Those three numbers point to one of three problems: not shown, not clicked, or not watched.

## Problem 1: people see the video but do not click

**1. Make the title promise one specific thing.** "My morning routine" is vague. "The 20-minute morning routine that fixed my focus" gives a reason to click. Put the most important words first, because long titles get cut off on phones. The [title analyzer](../../creator-tools/youtube-title-analyzer/) scores length, keyword position and clarity.

**2. Design the thumbnail for a phone.** Most viewers see your thumbnail at a few hundred pixels wide or smaller. One face or object, high contrast, three words at most. The [thumbnail tester](../../creator-tools/youtube-thumbnail-tester/) shows it at real feed sizes in light and dark mode.

**3. Make title and thumbnail work together.** The thumbnail should show what the title does not say. If they repeat each other, you have wasted one of them.

**4. Test alternatives.** YouTube Studio's A/B testing (on desktop, for long-form videos) shows up to three titles or thumbnails to different viewers and picks the one that earns the most watch time. A test can take up to two weeks, so use it on videos with steady impressions.

## Problem 2: people click but do not keep watching

**5. Deliver the promise in the first 30 seconds.** Viewers leave when the start does not match what the title promised. Skip the long intro and the channel plug. Show the result first, then explain how.

**6. Cut anything that does not move the video forward.** Watch your retention graph in Studio. Every sharp drop is a moment that lost people. Learn what happened there and avoid it next time.

**7. Structure the video so there is always a next question.** Tell viewers what is coming, and keep a reason to stay until the end. The [script outline generator](../../creator-tools/youtube-script-outline-generator/) builds a structure that does this.

## Problem 3: YouTube is not showing the video to many people

**8. Answer searches people actually make.** Search traffic is the most reliable source for small channels, because it does not depend on YouTube already trusting you. Use the [keyword generator](../../creator-tools/youtube-keyword-generator/) to find the exact phrases people type, and title videos to match.

**9. Make videos for one clear audience.** YouTube recommends videos to people who watched similar videos. A channel that jumps between unrelated subjects confuses that. Ten videos for the same viewer beat ten videos for ten different viewers.

**10. Make series and link them.** End screens, cards and playlists send viewers from one video to the next. A viewer who watches three of your videos is a strong signal to YouTube.

**11. Use Shorts as a trailer, not a replacement.** A Short that answers one question from a long video, with a link to the full video, brings new viewers to your catalogue. Shorts views alone do not convert into long-form viewers unless you give people a reason.

**12. Post consistently enough to learn.** Consistency does not trigger the algorithm. It gives you enough videos to compare and improve. One video a week for six months teaches you more than ten videos in a burst. [The best time to post](../best-time-to-post-on-youtube/) matters far less than this.

## What does not work

- **Tags.** They play a minor role. [Do YouTube tags still matter](../do-youtube-tags-still-matter/) explains how little.
- **Asking for subscribes at the start.** Viewers have not decided whether they like you yet.
- **Sub-for-sub and view exchanges.** The viewers never watch your content again, which teaches YouTube your videos are unwanted.
- **Reuploading the same video.** It splits views and can break the reused content rules.

## Track whether it worked

Change one thing at a time, and compare a video's first seven days against your channel's usual first seven days. To watch a channel's views and subscribers day by day, add it to the [growth tracker](../../creator-tools/youtube-channel-growth-tracker/).
