No platform replaces YouTube for long-form video that keeps getting found through search for years. But several are better at specific things: Twitch and Kick for live streaming, TikTok and Instagram for short video, Patreon for direct fan support, Vimeo for hosting video you sell or embed. Most successful creators use YouTube **plus** one or two of these rather than leaving it.

This guide is for creators. Every payment rule below comes from the platform's own help pages or announcements, checked in October 2026. Platforms change these often, so confirm on the official page before you rely on one.

## At a glance

| Platform | Best for | How creators earn |
|---|---|---|
| Twitch | Live streaming, gaming | Subscriptions, Bits, ads |
| Kick | Live streaming | Subscriptions and tips, 95% to the streamer |
| TikTok | Short video, discovery | Creator Rewards Program for videos over a minute |
| Facebook | Reaching an older, broad audience | Content Monetization program, by invitation |
| Instagram | Short video, community | Subscriptions, gifts, bonuses |
| Rumble | Long-form video | Creator Program, by application |
| Dailymotion | Long-form video | Creator monetization after a views threshold |
| Vimeo | Hosting, selling video | Selling through Vimeo OTT, no ad revenue |
| Patreon | Paying fans | Memberships, platform fee on earnings |

## Twitch

The biggest live-streaming platform after YouTube. Most creators start as **Affiliates**. In May 2026 Twitch lowered the Affiliate requirements to 4 hours streamed on 4 different days, an average of at least 3 concurrent viewers, and 25 followers.

The standard subscription split is **50/50**. Twitch's Plus Program raises it to 60/40 or 70/30 for streamers who hold enough paid subscriptions for three months in a row. Twitch also pays for Bits (viewer tips) and ads.

## Kick

A newer live-streaming platform built on a generous split. Kick says it uses a **95/5 split** on its main revenue streams: of a $5 monthly subscription, the streamer gets $4.75, and tips are split the same way. Its Partner Program has higher bars, including streaming hours, unique chatters, subscribers, followers and average concurrent viewers over 30 days.

## TikTok

The strongest platform for being discovered by strangers fast. TikTok's **Creator Rewards Program** pays for videos at least one minute long. To join you need to be 18 or older, have at least 10,000 followers and at least 100,000 video views in the last 30 days, and use a personal account rather than a business one. ## Facebook

Facebook merged in-stream ads, ads on Reels and performance bonuses into one **Content Monetization** program in 2024. It started as an invitation-only beta; creators can register interest from the Monetization section of the Professional Dashboard. Facebook reaches an older audience than most video platforms, which suits some niches well.

## Instagram

Instagram pays through **subscriptions**, **gifts** that viewers send on Reels, and **bonuses** offered to some creators. Gifts need a professional account, an age of 18 or over, and at least 500 followers. Most Instagram income still comes from brand deals. The [Instagram money calculator](../../creator-tools/instagram-money-calculator/) estimates what an account could charge.

## Rumble

A long-form video platform positioned as an alternative to YouTube. Rumble's **Creator Program** is optional, you apply from your dashboard, and creators are paid monthly based on performance. Rumble does not publish a simple fixed revenue split on its program page, and says eligibility can change.

## Dailymotion

One of the oldest YouTube alternatives. Dailymotion opens monetization once a creator's profile reaches a views threshold, and pays out once earnings reach $100. Its audience is smaller than YouTube's, so the same video usually earns much less.

## Vimeo

Vimeo is a hosting and business video platform, not an ad-funded audience platform. It shows no ads and shares no ad revenue. Creators earn by selling video through **Vimeo OTT**, their own subscription or rental storefront, where Vimeo takes a fee. Note that Vimeo's older **Vimeo On Demand** store shuts down fully on 20 November 2026.

## Patreon

Not a video platform, but where many YouTubers earn the most dependable income. Fans pay a monthly membership for extra videos, early access or community. Creators who launched on Patreon after 4 August 2025 pay a standard **10% platform fee**, plus payment processing.

## Which should you pick?

- **You stream live:** add Twitch or Kick, and clip highlights to YouTube.
- **You want to be discovered faster:** post vertical cuts of your videos to TikTok and Instagram, each pointing to the full YouTube video.
- **You have a loyal audience:** add Patreon or YouTube channel memberships.
- **You sell courses or films:** host them on Vimeo OTT or your own site, and use YouTube to bring people there.
