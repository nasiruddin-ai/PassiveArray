A **copyright claim** usually comes from Content ID, YouTube's automatic matching system. It does not harm your channel. It lets the copyright owner block your video, track it, or take its ad revenue. A **copyright strike** comes from a formal legal takedown request. It removes the video and counts against your channel, and **three strikes within 90 days can end the channel**.

## Side by side

| | Content ID claim | Copyright strike |
|---|---|---|
| Comes from | Automatic match against a copyright owner's files | A legal removal request from the owner |
| What happens to the video | Stays up, unless the owner chooses to block it | Taken down |
| Your channel | Not affected | A strike against your channel |
| Your money | Ad revenue can go to the owner instead of you | You lose the video's earnings |
| How long it lasts | Until it is released, expires or is resolved | 90 days, if you complete Copyright School |
| Three of them | No penalty | Channel can be terminated |

## What a Content ID claim does

Copyright owners upload their music, film and TV to Content ID. When your upload matches, the owner's chosen setting applies:

- **Monetize:** ads run on your video and the revenue goes to the claimant.
- **Track:** the video stays up, and the owner just sees its statistics.
- **Block:** the video is blocked, worldwide or in some countries.

The setting can differ by country. A claim on its own is not a strike and does not affect your channel's standing or Partner Program status.

## Disputing a claim

If you believe the claim is wrong, for example you own the content, have a licence, or your use is fair use, you can dispute it from **YouTube Studio**, under **Content**, then the **Restrictions** column.

- The claimant has **30 days** to respond. If they do not, the claim expires.
- If they reject your dispute, you may be able to appeal. They then have **7 days** to respond.
- While the dispute runs, revenue is held separately and paid to whoever wins. If you dispute within 5 days of the claim, revenue is held from the date of the claim.

Dispute only when you are confident. A claimant who disagrees with your appeal can respond with a takedown request, which brings a strike.

Often there is an easier fix: Studio lets you trim out the claimed section, mute the song, or swap it for a track from the YouTube Audio Library. That removes the claim.

## What a copyright strike does

A strike follows a valid legal takedown request. The video is removed and your channel gets a strike.

- **One strike:** you must complete **Copyright School**, a short course in Studio. The strike expires after **90 days** if you do.
- **Three strikes within 90 days:** your channel can be terminated, along with any linked channels, and you cannot create new channels.

Active strikes can also restrict features such as live streaming and can affect monetization.

## Resolving a strike

There are three ways:

1. **Wait 90 days** after completing Copyright School.
2. **Ask the claimant to retract** their takedown request. This is often the quickest route if you know the person, used their work by mistake, or can agree a licence.
3. **File a counter notification** if the video was removed by mistake or your use is fair use. This is a legal process: you give your contact details, which go to the claimant, and they can take you to court. Only do it if you are sure.

## The 7-day warning

Copyright owners can choose a **scheduled removal** instead of an immediate one. You then get **7 days** to remove the content yourself and avoid the strike. If you see this warning, delete or edit the video within the window.

## How to avoid both

- Use music you have a licence for, or tracks from the **YouTube Audio Library**.
- Do not upload other people's clips, films, TV or sports footage without permission.
- Commentary and criticism can be fair use, but fair use depends on how much you use and how much you transform it. It is decided case by case, not by a time limit like "under 10 seconds".
- Credit is not permission. Writing "no copyright intended" in the description does not change anything.

Claims and strikes are separate from demonetization for advertiser-friendliness. For that, read [YouTube demonetization](../youtube-demonetization/).
