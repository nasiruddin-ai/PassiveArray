Instagram engagement rate is the first number a brand looks at before a deal and the first number a creator checks after a post. It is also the number most often compared unfairly, because the typical rate falls steeply as an account grows. A 2% rate is excellent for one account and a warning for another.

## The formula

> Engagement rate = (likes + comments) / followers x 100

Measure it per post, then average the last 10 to 12 posts. One post that got shared into a large group will distort a single-post number.

Instagram has no free public API, so the calculators on this site work from the numbers you type in: followers, average likes, average comments. Copy them from the account's recent posts, or from Insights if it is your own account.

## Typical rates by account size

These are the benchmarks the Passive Array tools use. They describe what a healthy account of that size usually looks like, not a target you must hit.

| Tier | Followers | Typical engagement |
|---|---|---|
| Nano | under 10K | 4.0% |
| Micro | 10K to 100K | 2.0% |
| Mid | 100K to 500K | 1.4% |
| Macro | 500K to 1M | 1.1% |
| Mega | over 1M | 0.8% |

The drop is not a sign that big accounts are worse. A 5,000-follower account is followed by people who chose it recently and see most of its posts. A 2M-follower account is followed by many people who tapped follow years ago and whose feeds are now full. Reach as a share of followers shrinks with size, and engagement shrinks with it.

## How the grade is worked out

The calculator does not grade you against a single fixed number. It divides your rate by the typical rate for your tier and grades the ratio:

| Your rate compared with the tier's typical rate | Grade |
|---|---|
| Under half | Low |
| Half to 85% | Below average |
| 85% to 125% | Average |
| 125% to 160% | Good |
| Above 160% | Excellent |

So a micro account at 3.5% (1.75x the 2.0% typical) is graded Excellent, while a nano account at 3.5% (0.88x the 4.0% typical) is Average. Same number, different meaning, which is exactly what a brand comparing the two should know.

## Likes and comments are not equal

Likes are about 92% of total engagement on a typical account. That matters when you read a benchmark: an account with a normal like count and almost no comments is usually fine, but an account with a high like count and near-zero comments (under 0.3% of likes) is a pattern the fake-follower checker flags, because bought likes rarely come with real conversation.

## What moves the rate

- **Carousels and Reels** tend to earn more comments and saves than single images. Saves and shares are not in the public formula, but they drive reach, which drives the likes that are.
- **Posting less, better.** Engagement per post rises when the account stops posting filler. Total engagement per week may not.
- **Replying to comments** roughly doubles comment counts over time, because a reply is itself a comment and it invites the next one.
- **Giveaways** spike the rate for a week and then depress it, because giveaway followers do not engage afterwards.

## Using the number in a deal

For a brand, engagement rate is a screening number, not a price. Screen out accounts far below their tier, then price on reach and on the audience fit. The pricing calculator on this site starts from followers and engagement and adjusts for the tier, which is the same order of operations.
