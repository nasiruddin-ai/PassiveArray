The YouTube algorithm is not one formula. It is a set of recommendation systems that pick videos for each viewer on Home, in Up next, in Search and in the Shorts feed. YouTube says its goal is to **maximize long-term viewer satisfaction**, and that it learns from over 80 billion signals a day: what people watch, how long they watch, what they skip, what they search, and what they tell YouTube in surveys.

Everything below comes from YouTube's own help pages, its How YouTube Works site and its engineering blog. Where we add advice, we say so.

## The algorithm follows viewers, not videos

The most useful thing YouTube has said about its recommendations is that it does not judge videos in the abstract. It predicts what a specific viewer will be glad they watched. YouTube's own advice to creators is to ask "Does my audience like this?" rather than "Does the algorithm like my content?"

In practice, a video is recommended when people similar to its current viewers keep watching it and seem satisfied afterwards. A video that the right audience loves will spread. A video that gets clicks but disappoints will stall.

## How each surface works

**Home.** YouTube calls Home "primarily a personalized surface", and says it relies mainly on the viewer's watch history. It mixes subscriptions, videos similar to what they have watched, and new things it thinks they may like.

**Up next and suggested videos.** These use the video the viewer is watching as the main signal, then personalize from there. This is why videos on the same topic as a popular video can pick up suggested traffic from it.

**Search.** Results are ranked on **relevance** (how well the title, description and content match the query), **engagement** (signals such as how long people watch videos for that search) and **quality** (signals of expertise and trustworthiness on the topic). YouTube says how much each counts depends on the type of search.

**Shorts.** The Shorts feed is personalized to what YouTube thinks the viewer wants to see next, based on what they have watched, skipped and engaged with in the feed.

## The signals YouTube has named

- **Clicks.** YouTube's engineers have written that clicking a video is a strong indication a viewer will find it satisfying. Clicks alone are not enough, which is why clickbait does not last.
- **Watch time and how much of a video is watched.** Long-standing and still central, but YouTube says it measures satisfaction, not just watch time.
- **Surveys.** YouTube asks a sample of viewers to rate videos from one to five stars. Only videos rated four or five count as what YouTube calls "valued watch time".
- **Likes, dislikes and shares.** YouTube says people are more likely to be satisfied with videos they like or share, and that a dislike is a signal too.
- **"Not interested" and "Don't recommend channel".** Direct feedback that removes videos from a viewer's recommendations.
- **Searches.** What a viewer searches for influences what they are recommended later.

## Myths to ignore

- **"Upload at a set time to beat the algorithm."** YouTube says publish time does not affect a video's long-term performance. [Best time to post](../best-time-to-post-on-youtube/) explains when timing helps a little.
- **"Tags decide what gets recommended."** Tags play a minor role, mostly for common misspellings. [Do YouTube tags still matter](../do-youtube-tags-still-matter/) covers this.
- **"Deleting old videos resets the algorithm."** Recommendations are about each video's viewers. Removing an old video does not boost new ones.
- **"The algorithm punishes you for taking a break."** A break means fewer videos to recommend, so views fall. They recover when you publish videos your audience wants.
- **"Long videos are always favoured."** Satisfaction matters, not length. A tight eight minutes beats a padded twenty.

## What this means for your videos

This part is our advice, built on the signals above.

1. **Earn the click honestly.** The title and thumbnail should make a promise the video keeps. The [title analyzer](../../creator-tools/youtube-title-analyzer/) and [thumbnail tester](../../creator-tools/youtube-thumbnail-tester/) help with the first half.
2. **Keep the promise early.** Viewers who leave in the first 30 seconds tell YouTube the video did not match the click.
3. **Make videos for one audience.** Recommendations spread through similar viewers. A channel that jumps between unrelated subjects confuses that.
4. **Answer searches.** Search is the surface where a new channel competes on relevance, not reputation. The [keyword generator](../../creator-tools/youtube-keyword-generator/) shows what people type.
5. **Judge by your audience.** If your regular viewers come back and watch to the end, the systems will find more people like them.
