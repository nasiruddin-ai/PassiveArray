No, Instagram does not pay creators a rate per view for their reels. There is **no open program** that pays every creator for reel plays. The only Instagram payout tied to how a reel performs is **bonuses**, and Instagram says they are "limited time and invitation-only". A reel can still earn in three other ways: **gifts** from viewers, worth **$0.01 for every Star** you receive; **brand deals and partnership ads**, which the brand pays you directly, off the platform; and, if Facebook invites you into its **Content Monetization** program, the reels you cross-post to Facebook.

This guide goes through each of those, with what Meta's own pages say today, who qualifies, and which programs have ended. It does not give per-view dollar rates, because Instagram does not publish any. For the income most creators actually rely on, the [Instagram money calculator](../../creator-tools/instagram-money-calculator/) estimates what a sponsored post is worth from your followers and engagement rate.

## How a reel can earn: the short answer

| Way a reel earns | Who pays | Open to everyone? | Main rules |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bonuses | Instagram | No, invitation only | Professional account, Partner Monetization Policies, a country where a bonus is being tested |
| Gifts | Viewers buy Stars; Instagram pays you $0.01 per Star | Eligible creators | 18+, 500 followers, professional account, one of 49 countries |
| Sponsored reels | The brand, directly | Anyone a brand hires | Tag the brand with the paid partnership label |
| Partnership ads | The brand, directly | Only with your permission | Professional account, established presence |
| Cross-posting to Facebook | Facebook | No, invitation only | Facebook's Content Monetization rules |

Instagram also has subscriptions and badges in Live, which are not tied to individual reels. Our guide to [how Instagram creators make money](../how-do-instagram-creators-make-money/) covers every income stream; this article goes deeper on reels only.

## Does Instagram pay per view? What changed and when

Instagram has tried paying for reel plays several times. Each attempt was a limited test.

- **Reels Play bonus, ended March 2023.** Meta's creators site says: "Beginning on March 9, 2023, we stopped extending new and renewed Reels Play deals for creators on Instagram and Facebook globally."
- **Holiday, New Year's and spring bonuses, November 2023 to spring 2024.** Meta's Newsroom announced "an invite-only holiday bonus that rewards creators for reels plays and photo views on Instagram". It was renewed in January and April 2024, and Meta said it "remains an invite-only program in the United States, South Korea, and Japan."
- **Today.** Instagram's Help Center says: "At this time, we're testing bonuses on Instagram." It adds: "We will announce when we are ready to offer public eligibility for a bonus program." We found no such announcement on Meta's pages as of 19 November 2026.

What about ads inside reels? In February 2022, Instagram's creators blog said it would "begin testing a new ad experience on Instagram, which will allow creators to earn revenue from ads displayed on their reels." Today, Instagram's payouts page lists the tools that pay out as badges, bonuses, gifts and subscriptions, and we could not find a current Help Center page for a reels ad revenue share. Our reading: do not plan around it.

## 1. Bonuses: the only payout tied to reel performance

Instagram's creators site says "Bonuses reward you for the performance of your reels and photos, including carousels and single-image posts." You cannot apply. To be eligible for an invitation, the Help Center says you must:

1. Have a professional account (business or creator).
2. Meet the Partner Monetization Policies.
3. "Live in an eligible country where we are testing bonuses."

Even then, there is "an element of random selection due to the nature of product testing". Invitations appear in your professional dashboard, and may also come as an app notification or an email from Meta. Respond in time: "Your eligibility may expire if you do not respond to the invitation by the end of the deal period."

Set up payouts in time: you "must have a payout account set up before the bonus period ends." And watch for fake invitations: "Authentic invitations will not ask you to provide additional personal information, such as your Instagram username or password."

Bonus payouts arrive around the 7th and 21st of each month, each covering a two-week period of the previous month. The current Help pages do not list which countries are being tested.

## 2. Gifts on reels

Gifts are the one Instagram payout any eligible creator can switch on for their reels. Viewers buy **Stars** in the app and send virtual gifts on your reels. "Only creators can see the specific gifts they receive."

### What a creator receives

The Help Center: "you'll receive $.01 USD for every Star received." So 1,000 Stars are worth $10 to you. Instagram pays this monthly.

That is not the same as what the viewer spent. Instagram "takes a share of revenue at the time a viewer purchases Stars", which "will vary depending on the size of the Stars pack", and the app stores take in-app purchase fees that "are typically 30%". Your rate per Star stays fixed at $0.01 either way.

### Who can earn from gifts

The Help Center lists these requirements:

1. A professional account.
2. Aged 18 or older.
3. At least 500 followers.
4. Meet the Partner Monetization Policies, and stay within the Community Standards and Content Monetization Policies.
5. Agree to the Instagram Gifts Creators Terms.
6. A payout account set up. Without one, you can see who sent a gift but not earn from it.
7. Live in one of the 49 countries listed, which include the US, UK, Canada, India, Brazil, Japan and much of Europe.

Two exclusions: "collaborative posts and accounts that primarily post content focused on children are not eligible". And the follower bar has dropped: Meta's November 2023 announcement said creators needed "at least 5,000 followers", while the Help Center now says 500.

Track gift earnings in the app: Professional Dashboard, then **Gifts** under Your Tools. Note that Facebook Stars on Reels is a separate product; Instagram says "You can't use your Facebook Stars balance to send gifts on Instagram."

## 3. Sponsored reels and partnership ads

For most creators this is where reels earn, and Instagram is not the payer.

### Sponsored reels

Instagram defines branded content as content "for which the creator has been compensated by a brand or business partner. This may include when products and services are gifted for free." When there is an exchange of value, Meta's policies require you to tag the business partner, which adds a "Paid partnership with" label. A reel with an affiliate link "should have the Paid partnership with label, but does not need to have a business partner tagged."

### Partnership ads

"Branded content ads are now called partnership ads." A partnership ad is a brand's ad that runs from your handle. The brand needs your permission, and "Creators and other partners can revoke these permissions at any time." There are two kinds:

- **Content-level:** for one post, story or reel. You turn on the **Allow brand partner to boost** toggle on a reel that tags the brand, or share a partnership ad code.
- **Account-level:** broader, for ongoing partnerships. The brand can then make ads from any of your posts that tag it, and even partnership ads "without pre-existing content".

Meta does not pay you for this. Its Help Center says "Creators who make content that is used for partnership ads are paid off the platform." Eligibility requires an Instagram professional account and "an authentic, established presence", which "includes maintaining a sufficient follower base". Meta does not state a number.

### How to price a sponsored reel

There is no official rate. Our method starts at **$10 per 1,000 followers** for a feed post, adjusts it for your engagement rate against your account size, and prices a reel higher than a feed post because it reaches beyond your followers. That is our estimate, explained in full in [how much to charge for a sponsored Instagram post](../how-much-to-charge-for-a-sponsored-instagram-post/). Measure your engagement first with the [Instagram engagement rate calculator](../../creator-tools/instagram-engagement-rate-calculator/), then let the [Instagram pricing calculator](../../creator-tools/instagram-pricing-calculator/) quote a reel, a feed post and a story separately. Quote partnership ad permission as its own line: it lets the brand spend money behind your handle.

## 4. Cross-posting reels to Facebook

Facebook does pay for reels, by performance, if you are in its program. Meta's crossposting page says: "If your account is eligible, with crossposting, you may be able to take advantage of monetisation programmes available across both platforms."

### What Facebook's Content Monetization pays for

The Facebook Business Help Center says the program "lets you earn money from your eligible public Facebook videos, reels, photos and text posts." Meta's March 2026 creators blog explains that "your approximate earnings are based on the qualified views that your content receives." Its list of views that do not qualify includes repeat views, views under five seconds, and content shorter than 10 seconds.

On cross-posts specifically, two official statements matter:

- Views on content cross-posted from another person's Page do not qualify, but "This doesn't apply to content crossposted from Instagram."
- Facebook's Creator Content Monetization Terms: "Content shared from Instagram that is shown as recommended content on Facebook does not count as progress towards fulfilling a Content Monetisation Offer."

Our reading, not a Meta ruling: a reel you cross-post to your own Facebook profile or Page can earn like any other Facebook reel, but Instagram content that Facebook merely recommends does not count towards a monetization offer.

### The catch: it is invitation-only

"While content monetisation is currently an invitation-only programme, you can express your interest by visiting your professional dashboard on the Facebook mobile app, selecting the Monetisation tab and then choosing Content monetisation to complete the interest form." Facebook's older programs ended: "In-stream ads, Ads on Reels and the Performance bonus programme ended on 31 August 2025."

### Facebook programs aimed at creators from other apps

- **Creator Fast Track**, announced 18 March 2026, gives "three months of guaranteed pay for sharing eligible reels on Facebook": $1,000 a month for creators with at least 100,000 followers on Instagram, TikTok or YouTube, and $3,000 a month above one million. Meta's 13 November 2026 update added a College Edition for US college creators with 1,000 or more followers who apply with a .edu email, and lists $100 to $450 a month for creators with at least 20,000 followers.
- **The Breakthrough bonus**, a US-only program, "will no longer be accepting applications starting on 26 February 2026."

To switch on cross-posting, Meta's steps are: in your Instagram professional profile, open the menu, then **Settings and privacy**, then **Crossposting**, and turn it on for reels.

## The rules every reel payout depends on

Every Instagram payout sits on two policy sets. The **Partner Monetization Policies** cover your account: you must "reside in an eligible country", follow the Community Standards, have "an authentic, established presence", and monetize only "content consumed by their natural, authentic audience". Fake engagement can cost you the ability to monetize, which is one more reason never to buy views or followers. Our guide to [spotting fake followers](../how-to-spot-fake-followers/) shows what that looks like from outside.

The **Content Monetization Policies** cover the reel itself. These formats "cannot be monetized":

| Format | Instagram's description |
|---|---|
| Static videos | "one static image and little to no motion" |
| Slideshows of images | "static images played in succession" |
| Looping videos | "loops and displays the same segment multiple times" |
| Text montages | "still or moving images with overlaid text" |
| Embedded ads | Content that already includes ads where Instagram offers ad placements |

Unoriginal content is also out: "Content that is unoriginal or reproduced without making meaningful enhancements (commentary, parody, creative editing, etc.) cannot be monetized." Engagement bait, misinformation and some sensitive topics can reduce or remove monetization.

To check where you stand, open Settings, then **Creator tools and controls**, then **Monetization status**. You can see violations at account and content level and tap **Request a review** if you think Instagram got it wrong. Meta also says it can "modify, suspend or terminate access to" its monetization features at any time.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does Instagram pay for reels views?

No. Instagram has no open program that pays a rate per view. The only payout tied to reel performance is bonuses, which are invitation-only tests in selected countries. Most creators earn from reels through gifts, brand deals and partnership ads.

### How much does Instagram pay for reels?

Instagram publishes no per-view rate. The one fixed figure is for gifts: creators receive $0.01 for every Star viewers send on their reels. Instagram's Help pages publish no bonus rates, and brand deals are whatever you agree with the brand.

### What is the Instagram reels bonus?

It is an invitation-only payment for the performance of your reels and photos. Instagram says bonuses are limited time and invitation-only while it builds a sustainable program. The old Reels Play bonus stopped offering new deals on 9 March 2023. You cannot apply; invitations appear in your professional dashboard.

### How do I get paid for gifts on Instagram reels?

Switch on Gifts from your professional dashboard if it is offered to you. You need a professional account, to be 18 or older, at least 500 followers, a payout account, and to live in one of the countries Instagram lists. You receive $0.01 per Star, paid monthly.

### How many followers do you need to get paid on Instagram reels?

For gifts, Instagram's Help Center says 500. Bonuses are by invitation, and partnership ads need an established presence with a sufficient follower base, without a published number. Brands set their own minimums for sponsored reels.

### Does Facebook pay for reels cross-posted from Instagram?

It can, if Facebook has invited you into its Content Monetization program. Meta says views on content cross-posted from Instagram are not excluded from qualified views, but Instagram content that Facebook only shows as recommended content does not count towards a monetization offer.

### Why can't I see bonuses or gifts in my Instagram account?

Bonuses are only shown to invited creators. Gifts depend on your country, age, follower count, account type and the monetization policies. Check Monetization status under Creator tools and controls in your settings to see whether a policy violation is blocking you.
