YouTube Analytics lives in **YouTube Studio**, under **Analytics** in the left menu. It shows how your channel and each video perform: how often YouTube showed them, how many people clicked, how long they watched, who they are and, once you are monetized, what you earned. This guide explains each tab, what the key metrics actually measure, and which few numbers to check every week.

This is about your own channel. To see the public stats of someone else's channel, read [how to check any YouTube channel's stats](../how-to-check-any-youtube-channel-stats/).

## The tabs

At channel level, Analytics has these tabs:

- **Overview.** Views, watch time, subscribers and revenue for the period, plus **Realtime** (activity in the last 48 hours and 60 minutes) and **Typical performance**, which compares your latest video against your usual results.
- **Content.** Performance broken down by format: videos, Shorts, live streams and posts. This is where impressions and click-through rate live.
- **Audience.** Who watches: returning and new viewers, unique viewers, countries, age, gender, other channels they watch, and **When your viewers are on YouTube**.
- **Revenue.** Estimated revenue, RPM and CPM, shown once your channel is in the YouTube Partner Program.
- **Trends.** What viewers search for on YouTube, including your audience's searches over the last 28 days.

Each video has its own analytics too, with **Overview**, **Reach**, **Engagement**, **Audience** and **Revenue** tabs. **Advanced mode**, the link at the top right, opens an expanded report where you can compare periods, filter by video, country or traffic source, and export.

YouTube has been redesigning Studio gradually through 2026, so the layout on your account may differ slightly from this description.

## The metrics that matter

### Impressions

How many times YouTube showed your thumbnail to a viewer, on Home, in suggested videos, in search and elsewhere on YouTube. External links and embeds do not count. Impressions tell you how much YouTube is offering your video to people.

### Impressions click-through rate (CTR)

The share of impressions that turned into a view. YouTube says half of all channels and videos have an impressions CTR between **2% and 10%**. CTR usually drops as a video reaches wider audiences who know you less, so a falling CTR on a growing video is normal. Compare CTR between your own videos, not with other channels. The [title analyzer](../../creator-tools/youtube-title-analyzer/) and [thumbnail tester](../../creator-tools/youtube-thumbnail-tester/) help with the two things that drive it.

### Average view duration and average percentage viewed

Average view duration is the average minutes watched by people who stayed to watch. Average percentage viewed is the same thing as a share of the video's length. For Shorts both are calculated from engaged views. Use the **retention graph** on each video to see exactly where people leave.

### Returning, new and unique viewers

**Unique viewers** is an estimate of how many different people watched, counting someone once across devices. **Returning viewers** watched your channel before; **new viewers** did not. YouTube now also splits returning viewers into casual viewers (who watched in 1 to 5 months of the past year) and regular viewers (6 months or more). A growing number of regular viewers is the strongest sign of a healthy channel.

### Traffic sources

In the Content tab or Advanced mode, traffic sources show where views came from: Browse features (Home and subscriptions), Suggested videos, YouTube search, Shorts feed, external, playlists and channel pages. If one source falls, you know where to look. Search traffic means your title answers what people type; suggested traffic means your video works next to others in your niche.

### Views

Since 24 August 2026 YouTube counts a view the moment a video starts to play, across all formats. Monetization still uses engaged views and engaged watch hours. [How YouTube counts views](../how-youtube-counts-views/) explains the difference.

## Five numbers to check every week

1. **Views and watch time this week against your typical week.** Use the Typical performance card.
2. **CTR on your last three videos.** If one is well below your others, change its title or thumbnail.
3. **The first 30 seconds of the retention graph** on your latest video. A steep drop means the opening did not match the title.
4. **Returning viewers.** Rising means people come back for you, not just one video.
5. **Top traffic source for your newest video.** It tells you whether YouTube is recommending it or only your subscribers watched.

## What analytics cannot tell you

Analytics measures behaviour, not reasons. A low CTR could be the thumbnail, the title or simply a topic your audience does not want. Change one thing at a time and compare.

It also only shows your own channel. To compare yourself with others, use public numbers: the [growth tracker](../../creator-tools/youtube-channel-growth-tracker/) records any channel's subscribers and views daily, and the [engagement rate calculator](../../creator-tools/youtube-engagement-rate-calculator/) compares likes and comments per view.
