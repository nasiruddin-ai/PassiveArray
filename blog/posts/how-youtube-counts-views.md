YouTube does not publish an exact formula for a view, and it is deliberately vague so the system is harder to game. What it does say, and what creators can observe, is enough to answer most questions.

## What counts as a view

YouTube's help pages describe a view as a viewer intentionally starting a video on their device and watching it. The key word is intentionally. Views are meant to measure real people choosing to watch, so YouTube filters out playback it judges to be automated, forced or repeated in a way no real viewer would.

There is no published minimum watch time for long-form videos. The often-repeated "30 seconds" rule comes from how certain ad views were once counted, and YouTube has never confirmed it as the rule for ordinary views.

## Views YouTube removes

- **Bots and scripts.** Traffic from automated programs is discarded, sometimes days later.
- **Refresh loops.** One person reloading a video over and over does not add a view each time. Some replays count; endless ones do not.
- **Bought views.** Views from services that sell them are a common source of the drops creators see when YouTube's checks catch up, and buying them breaks YouTube's policies.
- **Forced playback.** Views generated by tricking people into starting a video, or by pages that play videos without the viewer choosing to, can be filtered.

This is why view counts sometimes fall. YouTube counts first, then reviews, and removes what fails the review.

## Do your own views count?

Yes, if you watch it like any viewer would. Watching your own video once from your account can add a view. Replaying it repeatedly to inflate the number will be filtered, and watching it inside YouTube Studio's preview is not a view.

## Shorts are counted differently

Since March 2025, a Shorts view counts each time a Short starts or replays, with no minimum watch time. That makes Shorts view counts higher and easier to earn than long-form ones.

YouTube kept the stricter older measure as **engaged views**, which is what Shorts eligibility for the Partner Program is based on. In YouTube Studio you can see both. If you are tracking progress toward [monetization requirements](../youtube-monetization-requirements/), engaged views are the number that matters.

## Why the count looks stuck or lags

- **Live counts update in batches.** A new video can appear to stall for a while and then jump.
- **Studio and the public page can disagree.** YouTube Studio reports with a delay; the public count updates on its own schedule.
- **Spam review.** A sudden spike from one source can be held while YouTube checks it.

None of these means anything is wrong. Check again in a day before worrying.

## Views on embeds and in ads

Views on embedded players on other websites count, as long as the viewer starts the video. YouTube also counts some views from video ads, depending on the ad format and how long the viewer watches, which is how promoted videos gain views.

## What the view count does not tell you

A view is the start of a watch, not a measure of whether anyone liked it. Two videos with the same views can have very different engagement and watch time.
